One year ago I started the Nano Collective as a side project around a coding agent called Nanocoder. I was writing it on evenings and weekends, with no plan for what it might turn into.

A year later, the Nano Collective is a small but growing team shipping open-source AI tooling across several projects: Nanocoder in the terminal, Nanotune for fine-tuning small local models, Sentinel for continuous security and code audits, get-md for document conversion, json-up for type-safe JSON migrations, and prompt-scrub for stripping identifying content out of prompts. The whole thing is community-led, not-for-profit, and built on a simple bet: that AI tooling should be local-first, privacy-respecting, and open for everyone, not locked behind a handful of platforms.

None of that was the plan when I started. The plan was just to scratch my own itch with Nanocoder and see if anyone else found it useful. People did, and they started showing up: opening issues, reviewing PRs, contributing code, writing docs, sponsoring infrastructure, helping me figure out what this could be. That is what turned a one-person side project into an actual collective.

The thing I'm most proud of isn't the projects. It's the people who showed up to build them. There is a real core team now, and a wider contributor community across several timezones, and the work is happening in the open on a public ledger. That is not something I could have built alone, and it is not something I would have wanted to.

I don't know exactly where the Nano Collective goes from here. There are more projects on the way, more contributors coming on, and a lot of work ahead to keep the model honest and the tooling useful. What I do know is that the people who are here are the right people to keep building it, and I am grateful to every single one of them.

If you have used any of our tools, contributed to them, sponsored them, or simply pointed a colleague at them: thank you. That is what the collective is.

Read more about how it all works: https://nanocollective.org
